This 2026 undergraduate summer research project examined whether menopause accentuates stroke-induced skeletal-muscle weakness in a rat model. The work was led by **John Al-Rachid** through a Biomedical Engineering–Kinesiology Summer Research Studentship.

## Research Focus

Stroke can disrupt the neural signals that activate skeletal muscle, contributing to weakness and impaired movement. Menopause introduces additional physiological changes that may influence skeletal-muscle structure and function. This project brought those two research contexts together by asking whether menopause changes the extent of muscle weakness following stroke.

The project contributes to a broader collaborative research program investigating how neurological injury, biological aging, and skeletal-muscle physiology interact.
