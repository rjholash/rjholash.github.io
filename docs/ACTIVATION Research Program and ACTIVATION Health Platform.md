# ACTIVATION Research Program and ACTIVATION Health Platform

## Research Program Narrative (2024–Present)

In 2024, I co-developed and co-led the ACTIVATION (Activity Capture To Investigate Voluntary Activity in Oncology and Normal Populations) research program with Dr. Joe Kenal. The project was conceived to investigate how mobile-health and wearable technologies could be used to objectively monitor physical activity, recovery trajectories, and health outcomes in both clinical and healthy populations.

Initial support was secured through a $20,000 McCaig Institute SPARK Grant. This funding established the foundation for a multidisciplinary collaboration linking kinesiology, orthopaedic surgery, oncology, rehabilitation medicine, and digital health research.

The ACTIVATION research program originated from a need to better understand real-world recovery and activity patterns beyond traditional laboratory and clinic-based assessments. Our objective was to create an integrated research infrastructure capable of collecting longitudinal activity and health data while supporting participant-reported outcomes across a variety of clinical populations.

As co-investigator, researcher, and technical lead, I designed the methodological and technical framework supporting the project. When external software-development efforts were unable to provide a functional solution, I assumed responsibility for platform development and led creation of the ACTIVATION Health ecosystem. This work resulted in a secure mobile-health platform capable of collecting passive activity data, questionnaire responses, and clinical research metrics through a University of Calgary-hosted research infrastructure.

The ACTIVATION Health platform evolved into a comprehensive digital-health research environment incorporating participant enrollment, informed consent, secure cloud-based data collection, wearable-device integration, longitudinal activity tracking, and administrative study-management tools. The resulting system was designed to support multiple concurrent research studies while maintaining compliance with protected health-data requirements.

The initial SPARK-supported research program subsequently expanded into a broader research and translational initiative. The ACTIVATION collaboration contributed to the successful acquisition of a $75,000 Alberta Spine Foundation research grant led by Dr. Fred Nicholls, extending the program into spine-surgery and rehabilitation research. The platform has also supported additional grant development activities and collaborative studies involving researchers at the Mayo Clinic and the University of Toronto.

Beyond funding outcomes, the ACTIVATION program created opportunities for trainee development, interdisciplinary collaboration, and research translation. Multiple students contributed to software development, research implementation, and project growth through collaborations with the Hunter Hub and entrepreneurial programming.

The project was subsequently accepted into the Alberta SPARK Accelerator Program, which provided mentorship and support related to innovation, partnerships, commercialization, and translational research development. These activities broadened the impact of the project while maintaining its foundation as an academic research platform.

By 2026, ACTIVATION Health had progressed from a grant-funded research concept into a publicly available digital-health platform distributed through the Apple App Store and supported by a robust research infrastructure. The platform continues to support longitudinal health research, trainee projects, external collaborations, future grant applications, and discussions related to multi-institutional research deployment.

Importantly, the ACTIVATION program demonstrates how targeted seed funding can be leveraged into a sustainable research initiative that supports new collaborations, trainee development, additional research funding, digital-health infrastructure, and translational impact.
